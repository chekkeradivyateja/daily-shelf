## Understanding the Sourness Spectrum
Nothing kills the morning vibe quite like a shot of espresso that tastes like battery acid or unripe fruit. In barista terminology, this is under-extraction. It happens when water passes through the coffee bed too quickly, failing to dissolve the desirable sugars and complex flavors, leaving behind only the sharp acids and bitter compounds that extract early. Before you buy a new machine, you need to diagnose the cause. Is your brew time under 25 seconds? Is the shot dribbling out in thin, fast streams? If so, your water is moving through the puck too fast.

## Adjusting Grind Size: The Primary Lever
The most effective way to slow down flow is to increase resistance in the coffee bed. For most machines, this means grinding finer. If you are using a burr grinder, make small increments. Move the dial just one or two clicks finer and pull another test shot. You are looking for a shot time between 25 and 30 seconds from the moment you start the pump to the end of the liquid stream. If the shot is still sour after adjusting the grind, check your tamping pressure. While tamping force is often overhyped, inconsistency is real. Ensure you are applying even, firm pressure across the entire surface of the puck. A tilted puck creates a channel where water rushes through, causing localized under-extraction.

## Dosing and Distribution
Another common culprit is uneven distribution. If you scoop beans directly into the portafilter or tap it violently, you create voids. These voids act like highways for water, leading to channeling. Instead, use a distribution tool or gently rotate the portafilter to level the grounds before tamping. Additionally, consider your dose. If you are using too little coffee (under-dosing), there is simply not enough coffee to create the necessary back pressure. Aim for a ratio that matches your machine’s recommendations, typically around 18-20 grams for a double shot. If adjusting the grind and dose doesn’t solve the sourness, check your water temperature. Most home machines steam water between 90-96°C. If your machine is running cold, it cannot extract the full flavor profile. Ensure your machine has fully heated up before pulling a shot, and consider flushing a little water through the group head to stabilize the temperature.

## When to Stop Troubleshooting
Fixing extraction issues is a iterative process. Change one variable at a time. If you change the grind and the dose simultaneously, you won’t know which change made the difference. Keep a simple log of your settings. If you find yourself stuck in a loop of sour shots despite adjusting grind, dose, and distribution, the issue might be a worn-out gasket or a malfunctioning pump that isn’t generating enough pressure.
