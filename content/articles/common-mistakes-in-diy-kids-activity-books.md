Designing a homemade activity book can be a fun project, but it is easy to fall into traps that make the final product frustrating for the child to use. Many well-intentioned parents end up with books that are too cluttered, have illegible text, or are physically difficult to handle. By understanding the most common pitfalls, you can create a smoother, more engaging experience for your little learner.

## Mistake 1: Overcrowding the Page
One of the most frequent errors is trying to fit too many activities onto a single page. Young children have limited attention spans and short arms. If a page is dense with small tasks, they may feel overwhelmed or give up. Best practice suggests limiting a page to one or two distinct activities. Use white space generously. It is not empty space; it is breathing room that helps the child focus on the current task. If you feel the need to fill the bottom of the page, consider moving that activity to the next page instead.

## Mistake 2: Using Fonts That Are Too Small or Decorative
While decorative fonts can be cute, they often become unreadable when printed in small sizes. If a child cannot read the instructions, they cannot engage in the activity. Stick to sans-serif fonts like Arial, Calibri, or Verdana for instructions. Ensure the font size is at least 14pt for body text and 24pt for titles. Always do a print test to see how the text looks on paper, as it often appears larger on a screen than in reality.

## Mistake 3: Low-Resolution Images
Using blurry or pixelated images is a major turn-off. If you are using clip art from the internet, ensure you are downloading the highest resolution available. Low-resolution images will look jagged and unprofessional when printed, which can distract from the learning objective. If you are scanning your own drawings, scan at at least 300 DPI (dots per inch). This ensures that the lines remain sharp and clear, especially for tracing activities where precision matters.

## Mistake 4: Ignoring Paper Weight and Size
Printing on thin printer paper can be a source of frustration. Crayons and markers can bleed through thin paper, making the backside messy and difficult to read. For activity books, use heavier paper (at least 80gsm or 20lb bond). Additionally, ensure your PDF is formatted for the correct paper size (Letter vs. A4) to avoid cutting off edges. A mismatched paper size is a common reason why activities get partially cut off during home printing.

## Mistake 5: Lack of Clear Instructions
Children need simple, direct commands. Instead of writing "Please match the animal to its shadow," try "Draw a line from the animal to the shadow." Use visual cues like arrows or example boxes to demonstrate what is expected. Avoid abstract concepts. The instruction should be visually obvious. If a child has to ask an adult what to do, the design has failed. Keep the language simple and the visual guidance explicit.
