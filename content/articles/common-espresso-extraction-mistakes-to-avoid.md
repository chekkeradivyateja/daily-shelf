## The Myth of Tamping Force
One of the most persistent myths in home barista culture is that you need to stamp the coffee with the weight of your entire forearm into the portafilter. In reality, tamping is about creating a flat, uniform surface, not compressing the coffee into a block of stone. Over-tamping can actually increase internal pressure, leading to channeling where water finds the path of least resistance. This results in an uneven extraction that tastes both sour and bitter. The fix is simple: tamp until you feel slight resistance, then stop. Consistency in the angle and force is more important than raw power. Ensure your tamper is perfectly level; even a two-degree tilt can create a channel on the high side, causing that harsh, astringent taste.

## Ignoring the Bloom and Pre-Infusion
Many home machines lack a dedicated pre-infusion stage, but you can simulate it. When hot water first hits the dry coffee bed, it causes a rapid release of CO2 and expansion. If you blast this with full pressure immediately, you can crack the puck or create channels. If your machine allows it, enable the pre-infusion setting. If it doesn’t, try starting the shot with a low flow rate if possible, or simply ensure your water is at the correct temperature. Cold water will not bloom the coffee properly, leading to a flat, lacking shot. Conversely, if your water is too hot, you will over-extract the bitter compounds on the surface before the inner flavors have a chance to develop. Aim for that sweet spot of 90-96°C.

## Neglecting Grinder Hygiene
A dirty grinder is a silent killer of flavor. Oil residue from the beans builds up on the burrs and inside the hopper, turning rancid over time. This stale oil coats the fresh grounds, imparting a cardboard-like or rancid taste that no amount of grinding adjustment can fix. Clean your grinder hopper weekly and run a cleaning tablet through the burrs monthly. Additionally, ensure your grinder is not retaining old coffee. If you are using pre-ground coffee, it is already compromised. For the best extraction, grind immediately before brewing. Stale grounds lose their aromatic compounds and extract poorly, leading to a dull, lifeless cup.

## Overlooking Water Quality
Coffee is 98% water. If your water tastes like a swimming pool or has high mineral content, no amount of dialing in will save the shot. Hard water can scale your machine and alter the extraction chemistry, while soft water can under-extract. Use a filtered water source with a balanced mineral profile. If you are in an area with very hard water, consider using a water filter or bottled water designed for coffee. The TDS (Total Dissolved Solids) level should ideally be between 75 and 150 ppm for optimal extraction.
