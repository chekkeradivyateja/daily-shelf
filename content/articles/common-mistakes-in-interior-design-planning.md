## Ignoring Traffic Flow and Circulation
One of the most frequent errors in interior design is placing furniture without considering how people move through the space. If you are designing a living room, ensure there is a clear path from the entrance to the back of the room. A common mistake is pushing all furniture against the walls, which can make the room feel like a hallway and isolate the seating area. Instead, try floating the furniture in the center of the room, allowing at least 30 inches of clearance for walking paths. This creates a more intimate and functional space. Before finalizing your layout, walk through the room with your eyes closed to feel the flow of movement. If you bump into furniture or feel cramped, adjust the placement.

## Underestimating the Power of Lighting
Many homeowners rely solely on overhead lighting, which creates harsh shadows and a sterile atmosphere. A well-designed space uses a layered lighting approach: ambient light for general illumination, task lighting for specific activities like reading or cooking, and accent lighting to highlight architectural features or art. A common mistake is not planning for sufficient outlets or dimmer switches. Before you paint or hang art, map out where you need task lighting, such as under-cabinet lights in the kitchen or bedside lamps. This ensures that your lighting design supports your daily activities rather than fighting against them.

## Choosing Decor Before Functionality
It is tempting to buy beautiful furniture or paint a wall a bold color before considering the room's primary function. For example, choosing a bright white carpet for a high-traffic family room with pets is a recipe for stress. Always prioritize functionality first. Ask yourself: Who uses this room? How often? What activities take place here? Once you have the functional needs sorted, then layer in aesthetic elements. A durable, stain-resistant fabric for a living room sofa is more important than a trendy but delicate velvet. By aligning your choices with your lifestyle, you create a space that is both beautiful and practical.

## Neglecting Storage and Organization
A room can look stunning in a magazine but become chaotic in real life if you have not planned for storage. A common mistake is overlooking hidden clutter, such as shoes, bags, or office supplies. Before you finalize your design, audit your current storage needs. Do you have enough closet space? Do you need a built-in bookshelf? Incorporating storage solutions into the design phase is far cheaper and easier than trying to add them later. Consider multi-functional furniture, such as a coffee table with storage compartments or a bench with hidden drawers, to maximize space without sacrificing style.

## Failing to Consider Future Needs
Finally, many homeowners design their spaces for their current situation, ignoring future changes. If you have young children, consider how the space will need to adapt as they grow. If you are planning to stay in the home long-term, think about aging in place. This might mean installing grab bars in the bathroom now, even if you do not need them yet, or ensuring that doorways are wide enough for future mobility aids. By planning for the long term, you avoid costly renovations down the road. Designing with flexibility in mind ensures that your home remains functional and comfortable for years to come.

## Creating a Balanced Space
Avoiding these common mistakes requires a balance between aesthetics and functionality. It is about creating a space that works for you, not just a space that looks good in photos. Take your time, plan thoroughly, and prioritize the needs of the people who will use the space.
